# ADR 0014 — A `share` permission rung, and project-level share grants made real

- Status: Accepted (2026-08-13, explicit OK per CONSTITUTION §8.5)
- Date: 2026-08-13
- Deciders: Jasdeep (product)
- Relates to: ADR 0008 (grantable edit rights — this ADR amends decision 1 and extends decisions 3–5), ADR 0006 (expand/contract migrations — migration 0033 follows the same pattern), CONSTITUTION §9 (MVP scope — the sharing/roles line, and the external-guest hard rule, both updated by this ADR), CONSTITUTION §5 (permission lattice)

## Context

ADR 0008 made `edit` grantable and gave it exactly one capability: `uploadNewVersion`. Everything else — including re-sharing the document — stayed on `canManageDocument`, owner-or-org-admin only. That was deliberate at the time (ADR 0008 decision 1): a grant that also let its holder mint further grants was judged a bigger step than the ADR wanted to take alongside making `edit` grantable in the first place.

It stops being the right call once a document has more than one legitimate re-sharer. The Phase 20 sync CLI (ADR 0008's original motivating case) puts multiple developers on the same document via per-developer API keys; the natural next step — a developer who has edit rights bringing a third teammate onto the document without going through the owner or an admin — has no path today. The owner can grant `edit`, but an `edit` holder cannot then extend access further, so every re-share still funnels through one person regardless of how many editors a document has. The fix is the same shape ADR 0008 used for `uploadNewVersion`: a narrow, named capability on the lattice rather than a widening of `canManageDocument`.

Separately, the domain type `GrantSubject` (`packages/domain/src/entities.ts`) has carried a `{type: 'project'; id: ProjectId}` arm since the original schema, and `share_grants`'s own `subject_type in ('document', 'project')` check has allowed a project-subject row since migration `0001_core_schema.sql`. Nothing ever wrote one, and — more importantly — all six SQL access predicates in `packages/persistence/src/repositories/pg-repositories.ts` hard-filtered `subject_type = 'document'`, so a project grant inserted by hand would have granted zero access. A project is the unit a growing document set actually gets organized into (workspace tree, doc switcher, per-project cap in `tier.ts`), and sharing one document at a time inside a project of hundreds does not scale to how the product is actually used. This ADR makes the existing arm real rather than adding a new one.

Both changes touch the same surface (the permission lattice and the grant-creation/access-check code paths) and were designed and shipped together.

## Decision

### 1. `share` is a new rung between `comment` and `edit`, and it is narrow

`PERMISSIONS` (`packages/domain/src/permission.ts`) becomes `['read', 'comment', 'share', 'edit']`. A `share` grant buys exactly one capability: creating and revoking grants on the document it applies to — re-sharing. That is the whole list. It does not buy `uploadNewVersion`, and it does not buy anything gated by `canManageDocument` (delete, archive, move, resolve, review requests, suggestion accept/reject). The narrowness is enforced the same way ADR 0008 enforced `canEditDocument`'s: a dedicated predicate, `canShareDocument(actor, document, grant)` in `packages/app/src/policy.ts`, sitting next to — not merged into — `canManageDocument` and `canEditDocument`. Nothing that gates on management or on upload today silently changes meaning; a new capability got a new predicate, exactly as decision 1 of ADR 0008 required for `edit`.

### 2. `edit` inherits `share` by true lattice inheritance — an amendment of ADR 0008 decision 1

`permits('edit', 'share') === true`. Because the four rungs are ranked (`rank = { read: 0, comment: 1, share: 2, edit: 3 }`) and `permits` is a `>=` comparison over that rank, inserting `share` beneath `edit` means every existing `edit` grantee gains the ability to re-share the moment this ships, with no data migration and no new grant row — the lattice does the work. `canShareDocument` calls `permits(grant.permission, 'share')` rather than checking `grant.permission === 'share'`, so it is true for both `share` and `edit` holders.

This directly amends ADR 0008 decision 1, which said "[`edit`] explicitly does not buy: ... re-share the document (share link, user grant, or guest share)." That sentence is no longer accurate. The amendment is deliberate, not an oversight discovered later: once `share` exists as a rung, the alternative — `edit` sitting at the top of the lattice but _not_ inheriting the rung directly beneath it — would mean the lattice's own ordering lied about what a higher rung bought, which is a worse property than the one ADR 0008 was protecting. `docs/adr/0008-grantable-edit-rights.md` carries a pointer note under its header recording this amendment; ADR 0008's original prose is otherwise untouched, as the historical record of what was decided and why on 2026-07-30.

Document _management_ — delete, archive, move, resolve, review requests, suggestion accept/reject — is still not on the lattice at all, for either `share` or `edit`. Inserting a rung and having `edit` inherit it changes what the top of the lattice buys; it does not move the lattice's ceiling any closer to ownership. That boundary (`canManageDocument`, untouched by this ADR) is the one ADR 0008 existed to protect and remains exactly where it was.

### 3. Delegation is capped at the granter's own held level — editors may mint editors, share holders may not

A `share` holder may mint a grant of `read`, `comment`, or `share`, but never `edit`. An `edit` holder may mint any of the four, including `edit` — an editor can create another editor. This is enforced by a new domain function, `canDelegate(held, requested)` in `packages/domain/src/permission.ts`, wired into `createUserGrant` (`packages/app/src/use-cases/sharing.ts`): a non-owner/non-admin grantor's own held permission is loaded (`callerGrantPermission`), and a request exceeding it is refused with a new error code, `grant_exceeds_own_permission` (HTTP 403). Owner and org admin are uncapped — `requireShareable` returns `ownPermission: undefined` for them specifically so the cap never applies.

`canDelegate` is, as of this ADR, implemented identically to `permits` — both are "does `held` rank at or above `X`." It is exported as a separate function anyway, because the two questions are not the same question even though they currently share an answer: `permits(held, required)` asks "do I meet a bar," `canDelegate(held, requested)` asks "may I hand this out." A future change to one (for example, a rung that satisfies a requirement but is not itself delegable) would need to diverge without a call-site rename, and giving the concept its own name now is what makes that possible later without a silent behavior change hiding in a generically-named function.

The alternative — capping delegation _below_ the granter's own level, so an editor could grant `share` but not `edit` — was considered and rejected. It buys a real thing (a ceiling on how fast the editor population can grow: only the owner/admin can mint a _new_ editor, existing editors can only extend read/comment/share) at the cost of a second, asymmetric rule that every future rung insertion has to re-derive by hand ("what's the cap N-1 rungs down from the top," recomputed every time the lattice grows). The revocation model already provides the actual check on editor-population growth this alternative was trying to buy more cheaply: decision 4 below means the owner/admin can see and undo any delegation chain at any time, so an editor minting another editor is never unrecoverable, just visible. Given that the safety net already exists, the asymmetric complexity wasn't worth it for what it added.

### 4. Revocation is capped for non-owners — a share/edit holder may revoke only what they created

The document owner and any org admin may revoke any grant on the document, unchanged from before this ADR. A `share`/`edit` holder who is neither may revoke **only** a grant they themselves created (`share_grants.created_by = self`) — never a peer's grant, never one of the owner's own collaborators. This is new: revocation used to be owner/admin only, full stop, because only owner/admin could create a grant to revoke in the first place. `revokeShareGrant` (`packages/app/src/use-cases/sharing.ts`) computes `isManager = canManageDocument(actor, document)` and, when false, additionally requires `target.createdBy === actor.ctx.userId`, returning `forbidden` otherwise.

This has a real, accepted consequence, not glossed over here: revoking a grant does not cascade to grants created by the holder of the revoked grant. If Bob holds a `share` grant and grants Carol `read`, and the owner then revokes Bob's grant, Carol's grant is untouched — it is an independent row with its own `created_by`. See Consequences.

### 5. The guest and link caps become allowlists, not denylists — the most structurally important change in this ADR

Before this ADR, `GuestGrantablePermission` and `LinkGrantablePermission` were both `Exclude<Permission, 'edit'>` — everything except `edit`. That was correct the day it was written, because `edit` was the only rung above `comment`. It stopped being correct the instant this ADR inserted `share` above `comment`: a denylist that names the thing it excludes says nothing about anything added later, so `Exclude<Permission, 'edit'>` would have silently _admitted_ `share` onto both guest grants and share links the moment `PERMISSIONS` grew — no compile error, no test failure until someone wrote a test specifically checking the new rung, because the type still typechecked and the denylist was, syntactically, still doing exactly what it said.

Both are now explicit allowlists: `GuestGrantablePermission = 'read' | 'comment'` and `LinkGrantablePermission = 'read' | 'comment'`. An allowlist has the opposite failure mode to a denylist: adding a fifth rung to `PERMISSIONS` in the future does _nothing_ to either alias unless someone deliberately widens the allowlist, which is the direction a genuinely security-relevant cap should fail in — silently staying narrow, not silently growing. The runtime twins `isGuestGrantable`/`isLinkGrantable` were rewritten to match (`p === 'read' || p === 'comment'` instead of `p !== 'edit'`), and migration 0033 makes the database-level guest cap an allowlist too (decision 9). This same class of bug is exactly what CONSTITUTION §9's guest hard rule depends on staying closed every time the lattice grows again — it is the reason this gets its own decision section rather than a clause inside decision 3 or 4.

### 6. A project grant confers its permission on every document currently in the project, resolved live

`documentPermissionFor` (`packages/app/src/use-cases/sharing.ts`) resolves a caller's effective permission on a document as the highest of: any grant on the document itself, and any grant on the project the document currently lives in (`callerGrantPermission` filters the caller's grants to `subject.type === 'document' && subject.id === document.id` OR `subject.type === 'project' && subject.id === document.projectId`, then takes `highest()`). This is a live resolution, not a snapshot taken at grant-creation time or at move time: moving a document out of a shared project drops the access that project grant conferred, immediately, with no grandfathering; moving a document into a shared project picks up that access immediately, with no separate re-share step. The six SQL access predicates in `pg-repositories.ts` that decide whether a document surfaces in listings, search, and the home overview were rewritten the same way, each gaining an `or (sg.subject_type = 'project' and sg.subject_id = d.project_id)` arm alongside the existing document-subject check.

The alternative — snapshotting project-grant access onto each document at the moment it's added to the project — was not seriously considered. It would require either a background job that re-snapshots on every membership change (project grant added/revoked, document moved) or accepting staleness, and the live-resolution version costs nothing extra: `documentPermissionFor` already has to look up the caller's grants per request, and adding one more `OR` arm to an existing query is not a new cost center.

### 7. Project grants are org-admin-only to create, list, or revoke — deliberately narrower than document sharing

Document sharing (decision 1, and ADR 0008 before it) is available to the owner, any org admin, or a `share`-or-above grantee. Project sharing is admin-only, full stop — there is no analogue of "a `share` holder on the project can extend it further." `createProjectGrant`/`listProjectGrants`/`revokeProjectGrant` (`packages/app/src/use-cases/project-sharing.ts`) all route through `requireAdminProject`, which checks `actor.role === 'admin'` before it even loads the project.

The reason is spelled out in that module's own doc comment and is worth restating here because it's a real escalation path, not a hypothetical: `createProject` has no authorization check at all — any org member may create one — and a document owned by someone else can be moved into an existing project later by whoever has move rights on that document. If project sharing followed the document pattern (creator-or-admin, via `canManageProject`), a member could create an empty project, wait for someone else's document to land in it (their own move, or anyone else's), and then share the project — granting a third party access to a document they could never have shared directly by going through decision 1's document-sharing path. The document owner never consented to that access and the project creator never had standing to grant it. Admin-only forecloses this entirely: the only actor who can turn "a document is in project P" into "someone new can read that document" is the same actor who could already grant access to every document in the org.

### 8. Project grants are named-user only — never a link, never a guest

`createProjectGrant` takes a `GrantablePermission` and a `UserId` grantee directly; there is no project-link-grant path and no project-guest-share path in the codebase at all — not a capability that's typed narrower, a capability that does not exist. Two independent reasons, neither alone sufficient on its own to justify skipping the other: a share link is forwardable and a project's membership changes over time in a way a single static token can't track sanely (today's link recipient may not be someone who should still have access once the project's contents have turned over); and guests are structurally a per-document, per-share concept (CONSTITUTION §9 — an external guest is scoped to the document(s) they were explicitly shared) with no existing notion of "external guest of a project," which is an org-internal container. This ADR does not create that concept.

### 9. Migration 0033: widen the permission check (expand), tighten the guest check (also safe under blue-green)

`0033_share_permission_and_project_grants.sql` makes two schema changes.

First, it drops and re-adds `share_grants_permission_check` to `check (permission in ('read', 'comment', 'share', 'edit'))`. This is expand-only per Core Principle 6 / ADR 0006: widening a `CHECK` is additive, so every row the previous app version can write still satisfies the new constraint, and old and new application code run against this schema simultaneously during the blue-green rollout window. No contract step is needed or scheduled. In that window the old app version's wire schema still enums out `share`, so it never _writes_ the value; on read it passes an unfamiliar permission string straight through to a display label rather than erring, exactly the pattern ADR 0008's migration 0030 already established for `edit`.

Second, it drops `share_grants_no_guest_edit` (`permission <> 'edit' or grantee_email is null`) and replaces it with `share_grants_guest_read_comment_only` (`permission in ('read', 'comment') or grantee_email is null`) — an allowlist at the database level, mirroring decision 5's type-level change. Unlike the first change, this one is a _tightening_: it forbids `share` on a guest row, which the constraint it replaces did not. A tightening is not automatically safe under expand/contract the way a widening is, so it needs its own argument, and the migration file's own comment carries it: no existing row can violate the new, narrower constraint, because the constraint it replaces already forbade the only value that was ever outside the new allowlist (`edit`) on any row carrying a `grantee_email`, and `share` did not exist as a possible value in the column until the first statement in this same migration ran. There is no point in time, before or after this migration, at which a `share_grants` row with `grantee_email` set and `permission = 'share'` could have existed to violate the new check. The tightening is real (a future write attempt that the old constraint would have allowed is now refused) but it never invalidates a past write, which is what blue-green safety actually requires. It also covers the guest re-share path (`extendGuestGrant`'s in-place `UPDATE` of `permission`), the same as the constraint it replaces did for `edit`.

## Constitution amendments (applied with this ADR)

- **§9 core-scope line**: "roles (admin/member; grants carry read / comment / edit — ADR 0008, where `edit` buys new versions only and document management stays owner-or-admin)" becomes "roles (admin/member; grants carry read / comment / share / edit — ADR 0008, ADR 0014, where `share` buys creating/revoking grants (capped at the granter's own level) and `edit` inherits `share` plus buys new versions; document management stays owner-or-admin)."
- **§9 external-guest hard-rule paragraph**: "never document updates, never new versions, regardless of grant" becomes "never document updates, never new versions, never re-sharing, regardless of grant." The DB-check reference is updated from the retired `share_grants_no_guest_edit` (migration 0030) to `share_grants_guest_read_comment_only` (migration 0033), and the enforcement description now names both `canEditDocument` and `canShareDocument` as the policy-layer guest refusals.
- **`CLAUDE.md` hard rules**: the `share_grants.permission` bullet becomes `read | comment | share | edit (ADR 0008, ADR 0014)`, with `share`'s meaning (create/revoke grants on the document, capped at the granter's own level) added alongside the existing `edit` description.
- **`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`**: §7's permission-lattice line and the "grantable on named user grants only" line both gain the `share` rung and `edit`'s inheritance of it; a new line documents project-level grants (admin-only, confers access to every document currently in the project); the `share_grants` data-model line in §4 updates its permission enum and check-constraint name; the ADR index in §2 gains ADR 0014 (and, brought current in the same pass, ADR 0009–0013, which had drifted stale since ADR 0008 last touched this list); the migration-range note updates from `(0001–0030)` to `(0001–0033)`.
- No RLS change. No change to the tenant-isolation model: a `share`-permission or project-subject grant is an ordinary `share_grants` row under RLS exactly like every grant before it, unreachable across an org boundary by construction.

## Consequences

- **Delegation chains now exist and are not tracked as chains.** Before this ADR, every grant on a document was created directly by the owner or an admin. After it, a grant can be two or more hops removed from the owner (owner grants Bob `share`; Bob grants Carol `read`), and the schema has no notion of that chain — each row just carries its own `created_by`. This is why decision 4's revocation does not cascade: revoking Bob's grant cannot walk "everything Bob's grant caused" because that causal link isn't stored anywhere. The compensating control is that the document owner and org admin can always see and revoke every grant on the document directly (`listShareGrants` is unrestricted for them), including Carol's — so an unwanted downstream grant is never unreachable, just not automatically cleaned up when its origin is revoked. This is accepted, not a bug to be fixed later; building delegation-tree tracking to make revocation cascade would be a materially bigger schema and semantics change for a benefit (automatic cleanup vs. manual, but always-possible, cleanup) this ADR judges not to be worth it yet.
- **No transactional race protection on the sharing path.** ADR 0008's upload path reads the caller's grant inside the same transaction as the version write (`UploadTx.highestGrantFor`), closing a concurrent-revoke race. The sharing path introduced here does not do this — `requireShareable` and the delegation/revocation checks in `sharing.ts` read grants in one query and act on a separate one, so a concurrent revoke could theoretically race a share-creation or a revoke-by-non-manager check. This is a deliberate scope decision, not an oversight: the blast radius of losing that race is one grant row, and that row is fully visible to the document owner/admin (`createdBy` is on the row and in the API response) and revocable by them at any time. A new port or transaction type to close a race whose worst case is "one more visible, revocable grant row" was judged not worth it for this exposure, unlike the upload path, where the worst case of losing the race is bytes actually landing in a version.
- **A pre-existing bug found and fixed incidentally, not part of this ADR's design.** None of the six SQL access predicates being rewritten for project-grant awareness (decision 6) previously checked `expires_at` — only `revoked_at` — unlike `listForUser` and `highestGrantFor`, which already did. That meant an expired guest grant could still surface a document in the org overview/home screen and in search results after its `expires_at` had passed, until whatever path actually gated content access caught it later. All six predicates now carry `and (sg.expires_at is null or sg.expires_at > now())`, closing that gap. This rode along because those six queries were already being opened up to add the project-subject arm; it was not part of the `share`/project-grant feature design and would have been worth fixing on its own regardless of this ADR.
- `SharingError` gains `grant_exceeds_own_permission` (HTTP 403, decision 3) alongside the `guest_edit_forbidden` it already carried from ADR 0008. `ProjectSharingError` is a new error union (`project_not_found`, `forbidden`, `grant_not_found`, `guest_edit_forbidden`) for the three new project-grant routes.
- `features/edit-grants.feature`'s "Edit stops at new versions — management stays with the owner" scenario asserted `And "dev" cannot grant anyone else access to "spec.md"` — true under ADR 0008, false as of decision 2 of this ADR. That scenario needs inverting to match current behavior; as of this writing it has **not** been updated (still asserts `cannot grant`), and a separate pass owns that fix.
- An asymmetry users may notice, in the same spirit as the one ADR 0008 flagged for `edit`/suggestion-accept: an `edit` or `share` grantee can now bring other people onto a document, but still cannot delete it, move it, or resolve a comment on it. This is intentional (decisions 1 and 2) but is the kind of thing that generates a support question the first time an editor is surprised they can't also archive the document they can re-share.

## Alternatives considered

- **Capping delegation below the granter's own level** (an editor can grant `share` but not `edit`): rejected, decision 3 above has the full argument — asymmetric complexity for a benefit the revocation-visibility model already provides.
- **Snapshotting project-grant access onto member documents at membership-change time**, instead of resolving it live: not seriously pursued, decision 6 above — live resolution costs nothing extra over the query shape `documentPermissionFor` already needed, and a snapshot would need its own invalidation story for zero benefit.
- **Project sharing following the document-sharing pattern** (creator-or-admin via `canManageProject`, matching decision 1's document rule): rejected — decision 7 above, it opens a real escalation path (create an empty project, wait for someone else's document to land in it, share the project) that admin-only forecloses structurally.
- **Allowing `share` on project grants via link or guest grantee**: not seriously considered — decision 8's two independent reasons (link staleness against a project's changing membership; guests being structurally per-document) each rule it out on their own.
- **Cascading revocation through a tracked delegation tree**: rejected for this pass — see Consequences. The compensating control (owner/admin sees and can revoke everything directly) was judged sufficient without building chain tracking.
- **Closing the sharing-path race with a new transactional grant-read**, matching the upload path's `UploadTx.highestGrantFor`: deferred, not rejected — see Consequences. Worth revisiting if the blast radius of the race ever turns out to be larger than "one visible, revocable grant row," which nothing observed so far suggests.
